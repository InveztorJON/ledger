Privacy policy

Last updated: 20 October 2026

In short. Ledger reads your bank statement on your own phone or computer. The file is never uploaded, and Ledger's servers do not store your transactions. To use Ledger you create an account with your email and a password; we keep that, your free-trial dates and, if you subscribe, your Ledger Plus payment details. If you use the AI coach, a short summary of your numbers is sent to generate an answer and is not kept by Ledger. We do not sell your data and do not use advertising or tracking cookies.

Who we are

Ledger is a product of Jonspire Limited ("we"), a private company limited by shares incorporated in Nigeria under the Companies and Allied Matters Act 2020 and registered with the Corporate Affairs Commission (RC 9187600), with its business address in Lagos, Nigeria. We are the controller of the personal data described here. For privacy questions or to use your rights, contact jonspirelimited@gmail.com. If the law where you live requires us to name a local representative, we will list them on this page.

What Ledger processes and where

  • Bank statements you choose to upload. Read on your device to find dates, descriptions and amounts, in any of the currencies Ledger supports. The file itself is discarded after reading. If you use a password to open a PDF, it is used once and never saved. Ledger does not connect to your bank.
  • Transactions, tags, check-ins, quiz answers and progress. Stored in your browser on your device, only if you switch on "Remember my data on this device". Otherwise they are cleared when you close Ledger. We cannot see them and cannot recover them for you.
  • Your Ledger account. To upload a statement you create an account. We keep your email address, a salted one-way hash of your password (we never store the password itself and cannot read it), the date you agreed to these documents, the time of your last sign-in, and a random sign-in key for each phone or browser you are signed in on. We email you a 6-digit code to confirm your address and, if you ask, to reset your password. The code expires after 15 minutes.
  • Free-trial record. The 7-day free trial belongs to your account. We keep the date it started and how many statements you have uploaded (a number only: never the statements). This is what stops one person starting many free trials, and it works on any phone you sign in on. It contains no financial data.
  • Security counters. To block abuse (repeated guesses, mass sign-ups, bulk coach requests) we keep short-lived counters tied to a one-way hash of your network address, your email or your account. They hold no content and are deleted automatically within about two days.
  • AI coach questions (optional). When you ask the coach a question, Ledger sends your question, the recent conversation and a short summary of your numbers (monthly totals, categories and patterns) to our coach service, together with your sign-in key so we can check that your trial or plan is running. The service asks Anthropic's Claude model for an answer. It never sends your statement, individual transactions or the names of people you pay. Our coach service does not store or log the content of your questions. Anthropic processes the request to generate the answer under its commercial terms and privacy policy.
  • Ledger Plus subscription (optional). If you subscribe, we collect your full name, phone number, the proof of payment you upload (a screenshot or PDF of your bank transfer or your Whop card receipt, which shows details such as your name, bank, amount, date and transaction reference), your Ledger code, your subscription dates, and a count of statements uploaded in the current month (never the statements themselves). The email on your subscription is your confirmed account email. These are stored on our servers (Netlify) so we can match your payment, verify it, start and renew your month, remind you before it ends, and keep accounting records.
  • Payment checks. To help us check receipts faster, we may send your proof of payment to Anthropic's Claude model to read the amount, account, date and reference. A person at Jonspire Limited always makes the final decision by checking our bank account; nothing is decided automatically about you by software alone.

Ledger uses no advertising, analytics or tracking cookies and no third-party trackers. Your sign-in key is kept in your browser's local storage on your device.

Why we process it (legal basis)

  • Consent for reading your statements and, separately, for the AI coach, which you give in the app and can withdraw any time under Me → Privacy & data.
  • Contract for your account, your free trial and your Ledger Plus subscription: giving you access, taking and verifying payment, and sending service emails.
  • Legitimate interests in keeping Ledger secure and fair: sign-in protection, preventing repeated free trials and abuse, and keeping the service running. You can object (see Your rights).
  • Legal obligation to keep accounting and tax records for paid subscriptions.

Services we use

  • Netlify hosts the app, runs the account, coach and subscription services, and stores account and subscription records and payment receipts. Like most hosts, it records technical request information such as IP address and browser type.
  • Anthropic provides the AI model for the optional coach and for reading payment receipts.
  • Google (Gmail) delivers the emails we send you: confirmation codes, password resets, receipt confirmation, approval, renewal reminders and usage notices. It receives your email address and the message text. These emails never contain your bank statements or transactions.
  • Whop (whop.com) processes card and other payments made through our checkout for people outside Nigeria. It collects payment details directly under its own privacy policy; Ledger never sees your card number. It tells us that a payment was made, your name and email.
  • WhatsApp (optional): we may message the phone number you give us about your payment or renewal.
  • Google Fonts delivers fonts. It receives standard request information but never your financial data. The PDF and Excel reading libraries are served by Ledger itself.

We do not sell your personal data, and we do not share it for advertising. We do not make decisions about you by automated means that have legal or similarly significant effects.

Sending data to other countries

Ledger's providers may process data outside the country where you live, including in Nigeria, the United States and the European Union. Where the law requires a safeguard for this, we rely on the one it provides: for example adequacy decisions, standard contractual clauses or equivalent contract terms (including the UK addendum), or your consent. Before any data leaves your device for the AI coach, Ledger asks for your consent through its own switch, and you can use Ledger fully without the coach. Everything is encrypted in transit.

Your rights, wherever you live

Ledger is open to people in Africa, Europe, the Americas, the Middle East, Asia and Australia. Whatever the law where you live, we give everyone the same core rights over the data we hold about you: to be told what we hold, to correct it, to have it deleted, to receive a copy in a common format, to object to or restrict how we use it, to withdraw consent, and to complain. You can use most of these yourself: download everything held on your device (Me → Download my data), remove a statement, delete everything, or delete your account (Me → Your account → Delete account). For anything else, email us and we will reply within the time your local law requires, and in any case within 30 days.

If you delete your account, we remove your email, password hash and sign-in keys. We keep only your trial start date and upload count so that deleting and re-registering cannot be used to get another free trial, and any payment records we must keep for accounting.

Laws we follow, by region

Ledger can read statements and show amounts in the currencies of the countries below, so we apply the standards of each of these laws to our handling of the account and subscription data above. If you live in one of these places, these are the laws that protect you and the authority you can complain to. (This is a summary to help you find your rights; the laws themselves apply in full.)

  • Nigeria (naira). Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023, overseen by the Nigeria Data Protection Commission (NDPC). We will notify the NDPC and affected people of a serious personal data breach within the time the Act requires (72 hours for the regulator).
  • European Union and EEA (euro, Swedish krona, Danish krone, Polish złoty, Czech koruna, Hungarian forint, Romanian leu, Norwegian krone). The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), including the rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability and objection, and the right to complain to your national data protection authority. Norway applies the GDPR through the EEA agreement.
  • United Kingdom (pound). UK GDPR and Data Protection Act 2018; complaints to the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO).
  • Switzerland (franc). Federal Act on Data Protection (revised, in force since September 2023); complaints to the Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner (FDPIC).
  • South Africa, Namibia (rand, Namibian dollar). South Africa's Protection of Personal Information Act 2013 (POPIA), overseen by the Information Regulator. Namibia does not yet have a comprehensive data protection law in force, so we apply the same standards there voluntarily.
  • Kenya (shilling). Data Protection Act 2019; Office of the Data Protection Commissioner (ODPC).
  • Ghana (cedi). Data Protection Act 2012 (Act 843); Data Protection Commission.
  • Egypt (pound). Personal Data Protection Law No. 151 of 2020; Personal Data Protection Center.
  • Morocco (dirham). Law 09-08; Commission Nationale de contrôle de la protection des Données à caractère Personnel (CNDP).
  • Tanzania, Uganda, Rwanda, Ethiopia (shillings, franc, birr). Tanzania's Personal Data Protection Act 2022; Uganda's Data Protection and Privacy Act 2019; Rwanda's Law No. 058/2021 on the protection of personal data and privacy; Ethiopia's Personal Data Protection Proclamation No. 1321/2024. Each has its own data protection authority you can contact.
  • West and Central Africa (CFA francs: Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire, Mali, Burkina Faso, Cameroon and others). The national data protection laws of those countries (for example Senegal's Law 2008-12 and Côte d'Ivoire's Law 2013-450) and their data protection authorities.
  • Algeria, Tunisia (dinars). Algeria's Law 18-07 and Tunisia's Organic Law 2004-63 on the protection of personal data, each with its national authority.
  • Zambia, Botswana, Mauritius, Angola, Mozambique (kwacha, pula, rupee, kwanza, metical). Zambia's Data Protection Act 2021, Botswana's Data Protection Act 2018, Mauritius's Data Protection Act 2017 and Angola's Law 22/11, each with its data protection authority. Mozambique does not yet have a comprehensive law, so we apply the same standards voluntarily.
  • United States (dollar). There is no single federal privacy law for this kind of data. We follow the state privacy laws, including California's CCPA/CPRA (rights to know, delete, correct and opt out of sale or sharing, which we do not do) and similar laws in Virginia, Colorado, Texas and other states. You will not be treated differently for using these rights. Ledger does not connect to your bank, so it does not hold the data of a financial institution. Complaints can go to your state attorney general.
  • Canada (dollar). The Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA) and provincial laws; Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada.
  • Australia (dollar). Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles; Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (OAIC).
  • India (rupee). Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023; you can ask us to correct or delete your data and name a nominee, and complain to the Data Protection Board of India after contacting us.
  • Brazil (real). Lei Geral de Proteção de Dados (LGPD, Law 13.709/2018); Autoridade Nacional de Proteção de Dados (ANPD).
  • Mexico (peso). Federal Law on Protection of Personal Data Held by Private Parties (LFPDPPP) and its ARCO rights (access, rectification, cancellation, opposition).
  • United Arab Emirates (dirham). Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 on the Protection of Personal Data; UAE Data Office.

Where a local law gives you more than this page does, the law wins.

How long we keep data

  • Ledger's servers keep no statement data or coach conversations.
  • Data on your device stays until you remove it, switch off "Remember my data", or clear your browser storage.
  • Your account stays until you delete it. Sign-in keys last until you log out or reset your password. Confirmation and reset codes expire after 15 minutes. Security counters are deleted within about two days.
  • After you delete your account we keep your trial start date and upload count so a second free trial cannot be claimed (we review this at least once a year).
  • Ledger Plus records and payment receipts are kept while your subscription is active and afterwards for as long as Nigerian company and tax law requires us to keep accounting records (generally six years), then deleted.

Children

Ledger is intended for people aged 18 and over. We do not knowingly create accounts for anyone younger. If you believe a child has an account, email us and we will delete it.

Security

The app is served over HTTPS with a strict content security policy. Statement reading runs on your device and the AI key is held only on the server. Passwords are stored only as salted, slow one-way hashes; repeated wrong passwords lock the account for a short time; sign-in uses random keys that you can end by logging out. No system is perfectly secure; use a password you do not use anywhere else, keep your device locked and your browser up to date. If a breach puts your data at risk, we will tell you and the authorities as the law requires.

Changes

We will update this page and the date above when this policy changes, and show a notice in the app for important changes.
